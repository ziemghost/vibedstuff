Car Mattress Platform

A 15 mm OSB-3 top glued onto a frame of battens, to fill a 100 × 106 cm space that is 6 cm deep. All dimensions are in cm. Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom.

Good enough

It's more than strong enough. The battens sit on the car floor, so your weight goes straight down through them and nothing is really bending. The only span is the OSB between battens, about 41 cm, and 15 mm OSB-3 handles that easily under a mattress. At 22 kg/cm² the glue lines add up to roughly 40 t of capacity, so the OSB's surface layer would tear before the glue let go.

The things to watch are a floor that isn't flat and clamping while the glue cures. See the notes below.

Battens are 15 mm OSB strips on edge (1.5 thick, 4.5 tall). The frame stops 5 cm short of the board at the far end and 3 cm at the near end.

    Notes

    • OSB strips on edgeRip 4.5 cm strips and stand them on edge, so 1.5 + 4.5 = 6 cm. Glue each strip's narrow edge to the underside of the top. The load is pure compression, so OSB works fine here.
    • Check the floor is flatA car floor under a mat is rarely flat. If the platform rocks, glue felt or rubber strip to the bottom of the battens, or shim the low spots.
    • Clamp while the glue curesWood glue needs pressure. Two or three countersunk screws through the top into each batten work as clamps and hold the frame together if the glue ever fails from heat.
    • 5 cm end marginAt the far end the frame stops 5 cm short of the board edge, and at the near end 3 cm. The lengthwise battens are 98 cm long.

    For the Leroy Merlin cutting desk

    A standard cutting sheet in mm, in Polish, to show at the desk. Ask first what their minimum strip width is: some panel saws won't rip strips as narrow as 45 mm. If they refuse, buy ready-made wooden battens about 45 mm tall instead and only have the top cut.
